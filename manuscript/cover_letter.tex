\noindent Dear Editors,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, ``Thermoelectric transport in hexagonal CrN nanoribbons:
a tight-binding Landauer study of the Seebeck coefficient and figure of merit,'' for consideration
as a research article in \textit{Physica E}.

Chromium nitride is an established thermoelectric material, but only in its bulk and thin-film
forms; its behaviour in the low-dimensional nanoribbon geometry has not been reported. Interest in
low-dimensional CrN has so far centred on magnetism and spintronics---the predicted half-metallic
monolayer, the DMRG analysis of magnetic nanoribbon edges, and a predicted one-dimensional
ferromagnetic half-metal---while its thermoelectric response has remained unexplored. We close that
gap.

In this work we compute the spin-resolved thermoelectric transport of hexagonal (honeycomb) CrN
armchair and zigzag nanoribbons within a tight-binding plus Landauer--B\"uttiker framework,
deliberately lightweight and requiring no ab-initio computation. Our main findings are: (i) the
half-metallic character makes these ribbons intrinsic spin filters whose thermoelectric current is
fully spin polarized (a natural spin-Seebeck function); (ii) the figure of merit is optimised by
light electron doping to a sharp majority band edge, reaching $ZT\simeq0.2$--$0.3$ at room
temperature and rising with temperature; and (iii) we provide concrete design rules in terms of
edge type, ribbon width, doping window and temperature. A self-consistent mean-field treatment
additionally reproduces the magnetic edges of these ribbons and their exchange constants,
connecting our results, both in property and in method, to the recent first-principles work on CrN
edge magnetism.

We believe the manuscript fits the scope of \textit{Physica E} well: it concerns the electronic and
thermoelectric properties of a two-dimensional/nanoribbon system, employs transport methodology
standard in the low-dimensional community, and opens an experimentally relevant thermoelectric
material to low-dimensional design. It is complementary---in property (thermoelectrics versus
magnetism) and in method (analytic tight-binding versus ab-initio/DMRG)---to the only adjacent prior
work.

This manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration for
publication elsewhere. All authors have approved the submission and declare no competing interests.
The tight-binding/transport code and the scripts that generate every figure are provided in an
accompanying repository to support full reproducibility.

We thank you for considering our manuscript and look forward to your response.
