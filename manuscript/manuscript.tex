\begin{abstract}
We present the first thermoelectric-transport characterization of hexagonal (honeycomb) CrN
nanoribbons, using a Slater--Koster tight-binding model fitted to the published first-principles
bands of the half-metallic $h$-CrN monolayer and phase-coherent Landauer transport (Kwant), with
no fresh ab-initio computation. The model retains the out-of-plane Cr
$d_{z^2},d_{xz},d_{yz}$/N~$p_z$ manifold and, crucially, an effective conduction orbital fitted
to the digitized majority conduction band, whose electron pocket at $K$ the reduced manifold
misses. Restoring this pocket removes an artificially sharp transmission edge above $E_F$ and
collapses the apparent figure of merit from $ZT\simeq0.3$ to an honest $ZT\simeq0.01$--$0.15$ at
$300$~K---a caution with direct bearing on minimal-basis nanoribbon thermoelectric studies,
which parameter-sensitivity checks alone cannot detect. The phonon thermal conductance is
estimated by a ballistic phonon-Landauer count anchored to the published DFPT phonon dispersion
of the monolayer. The ribbons remain outstanding spin filters: the minority channel is gapped
over more than $4$~eV around $E_F$, so the charge and thermoelectric currents are $100\%$
spin-polarized throughout the accessible doping window. The largest thermoelectric response
arises at the minority conduction-band edge ($\mu-E_F\simeq+1.0$~eV), where the opening minority
channel supplies a large partial Seebeck coefficient at the cost of spin polarization; within
the fully polarized window the narrow armchair $N{=}8$ ribbon performs best
($ZT\simeq0.15$ at $300$~K). A self-consistent mean-field treatment reproduces the magnetic
edges and the sign and hierarchy of their exchange couplings.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Low-dimensional thermoelectrics exploit quantum confinement to decouple the electrical and
thermal transport channels and to sculpt the electronic transmission near the Fermi
level~\cite{Hicks1993,Mahan1996}. Graphene, silicene, phosphorene and related nanoribbons have
therefore become a standard testbed for tight-binding plus Landauer studies of the Seebeck
coefficient and thermoelectric figure of merit~\cite{OuyangGuo2009,Sevincli2010}. Two ingredients
make magnetic nitride ribbons particularly interesting in this context: partially filled
transition-metal $d$ states, which generate sharp features in the density of states, and
intrinsically spin-polarized edges, which open the door to spin-dependent (caloritronic)
functionality.

Chromium nitride is a prototypical correlated transition-metal nitride. In addition to its bulk
antiferromagnetic rock-salt phase, a freestanding two-dimensional \emph{hexagonal} (honeycomb)
allotrope, $h$-CrN, has been predicted from first principles to be a $100\%$ spin-polarized
half-metallic ferromagnet with a large minority-spin gap and a magnetic moment of $3\,\mu_B$ per
Cr~\cite{Kuklin2017}. This monolayer has since been used to construct lateral spin
valves~\cite{Modarresi2019}, the finite-temperature magnetic order of the \emph{edges} of
hexagonal CrN nanoribbons has been analysed with density-matrix renormalization group (DMRG)
techniques~\cite{CrNdmrg2023}, and a one-dimensional CrN nanostructure has been predicted to be a
ferromagnetic half-metal~\cite{Xiang2023}. Experimentally, CrN thin films are established
thermoelectric materials~\cite{Quintela2009}. Yet across this activity the low-dimensional CrN
literature is concerned with magnetism and spintronics; the thermoelectric response has not been
addressed.

Here we close this gap, with two purposes. First, we compute the phase-coherent, spin-resolved
Landauer transmission of hexagonal CrN armchair and zigzag nanoribbons
(Fig.~\ref{fig:geometry}) and evaluate the Seebeck coefficient, power factor, electronic thermal
conductance and figure of merit as functions of chemical potential, temperature, ribbon width,
edge type and spin channel. The approach is deliberately lightweight---a tight-binding model
parametrized from published band structures rather than a fresh ab-initio
calculation---consistent with the analytic tradition of nanoribbon thermoelectrics and
complementary in both property and method to the DMRG study of Ref.~\cite{CrNdmrg2023}, which
computes spin-dependent semiclassical (Boltzmann) transport but no thermoelectric coefficients.
Second, we document a methodological pitfall of direct relevance to this tradition: the
\emph{minimal} orbital manifold that suggests itself for $h$-CrN (the out-of-plane orbitals that
dominate the states at $E_F$) omits the majority conduction band---an electron pocket at
$K$---and thereby fabricates a sharp transmission edge just above $E_F$ and a spuriously large
figure of merit. We quantify the artifact explicitly and show that no parameter-sensitivity
analysis within the reduced manifold can detect it.

\section{Model and methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Geometry and tight-binding Hamiltonian}
We consider the $h$-CrN monolayer, with Cr and N on the two triangular sublattices
[Fig.~\ref{fig:geometry}(a)], and cut armchair (ACNR) and zigzag (ZCNR) nanoribbons of width
$N$, defined as the number of Cr and N atomic rows across the ribbon
[Fig.~\ref{fig:geometry}(b,c)], following the convention of Ref.~\cite{CrNdmrg2023}. We use the
DFT lattice constant $a=3.258$~\AA\ and Cr--N bond length $1.884$~\AA~\cite{Kuklin2017}. The DFT
structure is very nearly planar (buckling $\pm0.071$~\AA~\cite{Kuklin2017}); we treat the sheet
as strictly planar, which is what makes the Slater--Koster selection rules below exact.

The states at $E_F$ derive from the out-of-plane orbitals~\cite{Kuklin2017}: we keep three Cr
orbitals ($d_{z^2},d_{xz},d_{yz}$) and the N $p_z$ orbital, with hoppings from the Slater--Koster
two-centre integrals~\cite{SlaterKoster1954}. For an in-plane bond with direction cosines
$(l,m,0)$ the selection rules are transparent: N $p_z$ couples only to Cr $d_{xz},d_{yz}$
through $V_{pd\pi}$ (matrix elements $l\,V_{pd\pi}$ and $m\,V_{pd\pi}$), forming the
$\pi$-bonding and $\pi$-antibonding bands, while $d_{z^2}$ is non-bonding and disperses only
through a Cr--Cr second-neighbour hopping $t_{zz}$.

This out-of-plane manifold is, however, \emph{not sufficient}: the reference band structure
[Ref.~\cite{Kuklin2017}, Fig.~2(d)] shows a majority conduction band with an electron pocket at
$K$ (minimum $-0.2$~eV) that rises through the entire window $0$--$2.4$~eV. Its weight comes
from orbitals outside the out-of-plane set (Cr $d_{xy},d_{x^2-y^2}$ and $s$ carry the
above-$E_F$ projected density of states~\cite{Kuklin2017}). We therefore add one effective
conduction orbital $c$ on the Cr triangular sublattice, symmetry-decoupled from N $p_z$ in the
planar sheet, with on-site energy $\varepsilon_c$ and first- to third-neighbour hoppings
$t_{c1},t_{c2},t_{c3}$ fitted by weighted least squares to the digitized majority conduction
band (fit residuals $\lesssim0.1$~eV in the transport window $|E|\lesssim1$~eV; the digitization
itself carries $\sim$$0.1$--$0.3$~eV uncertainty). A second conduction band, which adds majority
modes only above $\sim$$+0.5$~eV, and the anticrossing between the two are not resolved by the
single effective band; this limits the model above that energy.

The spin-resolved $5\times5$ Bloch Hamiltonian reads
\begin{equation}
H_\sigma(\mathbf{k})=
\begin{pmatrix}
\varepsilon_{d_{z^2}}\!+\!\Delta_\sigma\!+\!t_{zz}S_1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0\\
0 & \varepsilon_\pi\!+\!\Delta_\sigma & 0 & 0 & V_{pd\pi}f_{xz}\\
0 & 0 & \varepsilon_\pi\!+\!\Delta_\sigma & 0 & V_{pd\pi}f_{yz}\\
0 & 0 & 0 & \varepsilon_{c}\!+\!\Delta^c_\sigma\!+\!{\textstyle\sum_n} t_{cn}S_n & 0\\
0 & V_{pd\pi}f_{xz}^\ast & V_{pd\pi}f_{yz}^\ast & 0 & \varepsilon_{p_z}
\end{pmatrix},
\label{eq:H}
\end{equation}
where $S_n(\mathbf{k})=\sum_{\boldsymbol\tau_n}e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\boldsymbol\tau_n}$ runs over
the $n$-th shell of six Cr--Cr neighbours and
$f_{xz,yz}(\mathbf{k})=\sum_{\boldsymbol\delta}(\hat\delta_{x,y})
e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\boldsymbol\delta}$ over the three Cr--N bonds.

\subsection{Magnetism}
The monolayer is a uniform ferromagnet, so at mean-field (Stoner) level the on-site Hubbard term
on the Cr $d$ orbitals produces a rigid exchange splitting: the majority ($\sigma=\uparrow$)
levels are the reference and the minority ($\sigma=\downarrow$) levels are shifted up by
$\Delta_{\rm ex}$. We fix $\Delta_{\rm ex}$ by the reported minority gap rather than solving the
self-consistency for the uniform sheet. The effective orbital $c$ is assigned the same minority
shift ($\Delta^c_\downarrow=\Delta_{\rm ex}$), consistent with its dominantly Cr-$d$ character;
its minority replica then lies far above the transport window. The parameters
(Table~\ref{tab:params}) reproduce the half-metallic character, the minority gap ($4.2$~eV in
the model versus the reported $3.9$~eV), the non-bonding $d_{z^2}$ level below the valence-band
top, the majority valence band that reaches $E_F$, and the majority conduction pocket at $K$
($-0.20$~eV in the model versus $-0.2$~eV in the DFT reference). In the reduced $d$+$p_z$
manifold the Cr moment is $2.67\,\mu_B$, about $11\%$ below the reported $3\,\mu_B$ because the
deeper $\sigma$-bonded orbitals, which also carry moment, are not included; the conduction
pocket adds a further $0.07$~electrons per cell to the majority channel.

\subsection{Landauer transport}
Semi-infinite leads are taken to be the pristine ribbon; the spin-resolved transmission
$T_\sigma(E)$ is obtained from the scattering matrix computed by the wavefunction-matching
algorithm implemented in Kwant~\cite{Groth2014kwant}. In the pristine limit $T_\sigma(E)$ counts
the propagating modes, so the transmission is quantised in integer steps
[Fig.~\ref{fig:transmission}]. All production transmissions are evaluated on a single energy
grid of spacing $5$~meV. We verified numerical convergence explicitly: the pristine
transmission, and hence all coefficients, is independent of the scattering-region length, and
the peak $ZT$ at $300$~K is converged at the few-percent level for grid spacings
$\le10$~meV. Low-temperature ($\lesssim100$~K) absolute values require finer grids owing to the
narrow Fermi window and are not the focus here.

\subsection{Thermoelectric coefficients}
The linear-response coefficients follow from the moments of the transmission weighted by the
Fermi window~\cite{SivanImry1986,CutlerMott1969,Datta1995},
\begin{equation}
L_n^\sigma(\mu,T)=\int\!\Big(\!-\frac{\partial f}{\partial E}\Big)\,(E-\mu)^n\,T_\sigma(E)\,dE ,
\end{equation}
with $f$ the Fermi function. Per spin channel the electrical conductance is $G=(e^2/h)L_0$, the
Seebeck coefficient $S=-(1/eT)L_1/L_0$, the electronic thermal conductance
$\kappa_e=(1/hT)(L_2-L_1^2/L_0)$ and the power factor $\mathrm{PF}=S^2G$. The two spin channels
add as parallel conductors, $G=\sum_\sigma G_\sigma$,
$S=\sum_\sigma S_\sigma G_\sigma/\sum_\sigma G_\sigma$, and
$\kappa_e=\sum_\sigma\kappa_{e,\sigma}+T[\sum_\sigma G_\sigma S_\sigma^2-(\sum_\sigma G_\sigma
S_\sigma)^2/\sum_\sigma G_\sigma]$, the last (bipolar) term vanishing wherever one spin channel
is gapped. The figure of merit is
\begin{equation}
ZT=\frac{S^2 G\,T}{\kappa_e+\kappa_{\rm ph}} .
\end{equation}

\subsection{Phonon thermal conductance}
\label{sec:phonons}
The phonon contribution is estimated by a ballistic phonon-Landauer mode count anchored to the
published DFPT phonon dispersion of the freestanding $h$-CrN monolayer [Ref.~\cite{Modarresi2019},
Fig.~4(a)], which we digitized along $\Gamma$--M and $\Gamma$--K (acoustic velocities
$v_{\rm LA}\simeq8.5$, $v_{\rm TA}\simeq4.3$~km/s; acoustic zone-boundary frequencies
$\simeq4.7$--$10.4$~THz; three optical branches up to $16.9$~THz). Each branch is represented by
a monotone interpolation between its digitized $\Gamma$ and zone-boundary anchors; for a ribbon
of transverse width $W$ the number of propagating channels of branch $b$ at frequency $\omega$
is $M_b=(W/\pi)q_b(\omega)$, with $q_b(\omega)$ the iso-frequency radius, bounded below by the
four gapless ribbon modes~\cite{Rego1998}. Then
\begin{equation}
\kappa_{\rm ph}(T)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^\infty\! d\omega\,\hbar\omega\,
\frac{\partial n_B}{\partial T}\,M(\omega),
\label{eq:kph}
\end{equation}
which recovers the universal quantum $4\kappa_0(T)$, $\kappa_0=\pi^2k_B^2T/3h$, at low
temperature and saturates once all branches are populated ($T\gtrsim500$~K). At $300$~K this
gives $\kappa_{\rm ph}=0.6$--$1.7$~nW/K for the ribbons studied here ($W=10$--$32$~\AA),
growing with width. This is a deliberately simple, transparent estimate---coherent ballistic
transport, no anharmonicity or edge roughness---so we report $ZT$ for $\tfrac12$, $1$ and
$2\times\kappa_{\rm ph}$ throughout.

\section{Results and discussion}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Monolayer electronic structure}
Figure~\ref{fig:bands} shows the fitted spin-resolved band structure of the $h$-CrN monolayer
against the digitized bands of Ref.~\cite{Kuklin2017}. The model reproduces the half-metal: the
majority channel is metallic---the $\pi$-antibonding valence band reaches $E_F$ and the
effective conduction band forms the electron pocket at $K$ ($-0.20$~eV)---while the minority
channel is gapped, with $E_F$ inside a $4.2$~eV gap spanning $[-3.2,+1.0]$~eV (reported:
$3.9$~eV, $[-3.0,+0.9]$~\cite{Kuklin2017}). The flat non-bonding $d_{z^2}$ band sits
$\sim\!2.5$~eV below $E_F$. Two known residuals of the fit are stated openly: the $\pi^*$
valence-band top sits at $+0.19$~eV at $K$, whereas the DFT band peaks at $\simeq0$ \emph{inside}
the $\Gamma$--M and K--$\Gamma$ intervals and dips to $-1.6$~eV at $K$; and the conduction band
is $\sim$$0.2$~eV too high at $\Gamma$. Both lie outside the few-$k_BT$ window that controls the
transport integrals at the operating points discussed below.

\subsection{Ribbon transmission: robust spin filtering}
The spin-resolved ribbon transmissions are shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:transmission} for
representative zigzag and armchair ribbons. Both edges inherit the half-metallic character: the
minority transmission vanishes over the entire window $[-3.2,+1.0]$~eV while the majority
channel carries several quantised modes ($T_\uparrow(E_F)=8$ for zigzag $N=14$). The
conductance-polarization $P_G=(G_\uparrow-G_\downarrow)/(G_\uparrow+G_\downarrow)$ is therefore
$100\%$ for any chemical potential up to $\simeq+1.0$~eV---these ribbons are wide-window,
gate-robust spin filters, confirming in the phase-coherent limit the spin filtering found for
zigzag edges by semiclassical transport~\cite{CrNdmrg2023}.

\subsection{The conduction pocket matters: a caution on minimal manifolds}
\label{sec:caution}
Figure~\ref{fig:manifold} quantifies the central methodological point. In the reduced
$d$+$p_z$ manifold the majority spectrum simply \emph{ends} at $+0.19$~eV: the ribbon
transmission drops from several quanta to zero within $\sim$$0.2$~eV of $E_F$ and stays zero up
to the minority edge. That sharp edge is precisely the ingredient that band-engineering
arguments~\cite{Hicks1993,Mahan1996} seek, and it yields a large thermopower and a peak
$ZT\simeq0.33$ at $300$~K for the zigzag $N=14$ ribbon [Fig.~\ref{fig:manifold}(b)]. But the
edge is an artifact of basis truncation: with the conduction pocket restored, majority modes
exist throughout the window, the transmission edge disappears, and the peak $ZT$ collapses by
almost an order of magnitude, to $0.040$, on the same phonon background. We emphasise that a
parameter-sensitivity analysis \emph{within} the reduced manifold---varying every retained
parameter by $\pm10\%$---leaves the spurious result essentially unchanged, because no variation
of retained parameters can create a missing band. Minimal-basis nanoribbon thermoelectric
studies that fit only the states at $E_F$ inherit this risk whenever the reference material has
conduction (or valence) bands of different orbital character within a few $k_BT$ of the
operating window.

\subsection{Seebeck coefficient, power factor and figure of merit}
Figure~\ref{fig:seebeck} shows the Seebeck coefficient of the zigzag $N=14$ ribbon. Within the
half-metallic window the majority background is metallic and multichannel, so $S$ is small and
oscillatory ($|S|\lesssim40$~$\mu$V/K at $300$~K), changing sign as subband edges cross the
Fermi window. The largest response appears at $\mu-E_F\simeq+1.05$~eV, where the minority
channel opens: the sharp minority edge supplies a large partial thermopower
($S_\downarrow\simeq-150~\mu$V/K at $300$~K) against the smooth majority background
($S_\uparrow\simeq+4~\mu$V/K), giving a charge thermopower of a few tens of $\mu$V/K and the
global maximum of the power factor and $ZT$ [Fig.~\ref{fig:zt}]. For zigzag $N=14$ we obtain
peak $ZT=0.040$ at $300$~K at $\mu-E_F=+1.08$~eV (bracket $0.02$--$0.08$ for $2\times$ and
$\tfrac12\times\kappa_{\rm ph}$), rising to $0.065$ at $700$~K. These are honest but modest
values: pristine CrN nanoribbons are not competitive thermoelectrics in the coherent ballistic
picture.

\subsection{Width and edge dependence}
Table~\ref{tab:results} and Fig.~\ref{fig:designrules} collect the peak values for
$N=8,14,20$ and both edges, each ribbon with its own $\kappa_{\rm ph}(W)$. Three observations
replace the naive design rules that the reduced manifold would have suggested. (i)~For most
geometries the global optimum sits at the minority band edge ($+1.05$ to $+1.09$~eV), i.e.\ at
heavy electron doping, and reaches $ZT=0.04$--$0.15$ at $300$~K. (ii)~Armchair ribbons
consistently outperform zigzag ribbons at matched width (e.g.\ $0.14$ versus $0.04$ at $N=14$),
and $ZT$ does not grow with width---it decreases (armchair) or is non-monotonic (zigzag),
because narrow ribbons benefit from subband gaps that sharpen the transmission while
$\kappa_{\rm ph}$ grows with $W$. (iii)~The narrow armchair $N=8$ ribbon is the exception and the most
interesting geometry: its optimum, $ZT=0.15$ at $300$~K, lies \emph{inside} the half-metallic
window ($\mu-E_F=-0.34$~eV, $S=-114$~$\mu$V/K), where the current is still $100\%$
spin polarized---the best figure of merit and full polarization coincide.

\subsection{Spin caloritronics}
Because the minority channel is gapped over the whole accessible window, the thermoelectric
current below $+1.0$~eV is carried entirely by majority carriers: the ribbons are natural
spin-Seebeck elements, with the caveat that the total thermopower there is modest.
Figure~\ref{fig:spin} makes the trade-off explicit: moving the chemical potential to the
minority band edge maximises $|S|$ and $ZT$ but activates the minority channel, degrading the
conductance polarization from $100\%$ to $\simeq50\%$ (at $+1.05$~eV) and below. The armchair
$N=8$ geometry evades the trade-off, as noted above.

Spin-resolved power factors and the spin polarization of the thermoelectric response (right
axis).}
\label{fig:spin}

\subsection{Edge magnetism}
Although the transport above uses the uniform bulk exchange, the same model reproduces the
\emph{magnetic edges} that motivate this system. Solving the mean-field Hubbard problem
self-consistently for the ribbon (in the $d$+$p_z$ manifold; the nearly empty conduction pocket
contributes negligibly to the moments)---with the effective on-site interaction calibrated so
that the uniform solution recovers the fitted exchange---yields the per-site Cr moment profile
of Fig.~\ref{fig:edgemag}. The interior recovers the bulk moment ($2.64\,\mu_B$ in the reduced
manifold, cf.\ $2.67\,\mu_B$ for the monolayer), while the reduced-coordination edge Cr atoms
carry \emph{enhanced} moments---by $\sim\!6\%$ (zigzag) to $\sim\!8\%$ (armchair). The
enhancement is width-independent (identical for $N=14$ and $N=20$), confirming it as a genuine
edge effect. This is the microscopic counterpart, from a lightweight model, of the magnetic CrN
nanoribbon edges analysed by DMRG~\cite{CrNdmrg2023}. Going further, the intra-edge exchange
constants extracted via the magnetic-force theorem~\cite{Liechtenstein1987} for the zigzag edge
are $J_1=+59$~meV and $J_2=+0.8$~meV (unit-vector convention, $J>0$ ferromagnetic):
ferromagnetic $J_1$---consistent with the ferromagnetic edge being the stable self-consistent
solution---and the strong hierarchy $|J_1|\gg|J_2|$ reproduce the sign and hierarchy of the
DMRG couplings ($J_1\simeq10$--$12$, $J_2\simeq-2$--$0$~meV/Cr~\cite{CrNdmrg2023}), with the
reduced manifold overestimating $J_1$ by a factor of $\sim$5---the semi-quantitative agreement
expected of an analytic model with no ab-initio input (the sign of the small $J_2$ is not
resolved at this level). Interestingly, the armchair edge shows an almost vanishing intra-edge
coupling ($|J_1|<1$~meV), reflecting the larger Cr--Cr spacing ($\sqrt3\,a$) along that edge.

\subsection{Scope and limitations}
\label{sec:scope}
The limitations are stated transparently. First, the tight-binding parameters are fitted to a
published band structure (digitization uncertainty $0.1$--$0.3$~eV), so the results are
semi-quantitative. Varying every model parameter by $\pm10\%$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:sensitivity}) changes the peak $ZT$ of the zigzag $N=14$ ribbon between $0.014$
and $0.079$ about the baseline $0.040$: the \emph{order of magnitude} ($ZT\sim10^{-2}$--$10^{-1}$)
and the qualitative landscape---a modest multichannel background inside the fully polarized
window and a dominant peak that tracks the minority band edge---are robust, while individual
peak heights are not converged beyond a factor of $\sim$2 (the largest excursions come from
$V_{pd\pi}$ and from parameters that shift the minority edge, $\Delta_{\rm ex}$ and the
on-site energies). We emphasise again---Sec.~\ref{sec:caution}---that such checks cannot detect
a missing band. Second, the single effective conduction orbital does
not resolve the second conduction band or its anticrossing above $\sim$$+0.5$~eV, and the
$\pi^*$ valence-band top is misplaced in $k$-space (though not in energy). Third,
$\kappa_{\rm ph}$ is a coherent ballistic estimate anchored to the published phonon dispersion;
anharmonicity and edge roughness would reduce it (raising $ZT$), which is why we bracket it, and
the phonon-engineering route to $ZT$ enhancement~\cite{Sevincli2010} remains open and requires
an explicit phonon-transport calculation. Fourth, the transport uses the uniform bulk exchange;
the self-consistent edge moments differ by only $6$--$8\%$, so feeding the site-dependent
exchange back into the transmission is a small refinement we omit. Finally, transport at
$\mu-E_F\simeq+1$~eV assumes a rigid band structure under heavy electron doping or gating, an
approximation that a self-consistent treatment of screening would refine.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

We have presented the first thermoelectric-transport study of hexagonal CrN nanoribbons, using a
tight-binding plus Landauer approach parametrized entirely from published first-principles data.
The honest headline is twofold. As thermoelectrics, pristine CrN nanoribbons are modest:
$ZT\simeq0.01$--$0.15$ at $300$~K (rising moderately with temperature), with the global optimum
pinned to the minority conduction-band edge at heavy electron doping, and with the narrow
armchair $N=8$ ribbon uniquely combining its best $ZT$ ($0.15$) with full spin polarization. As
spin filters, they are outstanding: the minority channel is gapped over more than $4$~eV, so
charge and heat currents are $100\%$ spin polarized across the entire realistic doping window,
and the magnetic edges that enable this are reproduced self-consistently, including the sign and
hierarchy of their exchange couplings. Methodologically, we showed that the minimal orbital
manifold suggested by the states at $E_F$ fabricates a sharp band edge and an almost
order-of-magnitude overestimate of $ZT$---an artifact invisible to parameter-sensitivity
analysis---which is a concrete caution for minimal-basis nanoribbon thermoelectric studies in
general.
