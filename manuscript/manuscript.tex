\title{Thermoelectric transport in hexagonal CrN nanoribbons:\\
a tight-binding Landauer study of the Seebeck coefficient and figure of merit}

\author[aff1]{Tomas Rojas}
\address[aff1]{\textit{Affiliation to be completed}}

\begin{abstract}
Nanoribbons of two-dimensional magnetic nitrides are attractive building blocks for
spin-caloritronic devices, yet the thermoelectric response of chromium nitride in the
low-dimensional limit has not been addressed. We compute the spin-resolved thermoelectric
transport of hexagonal (honeycomb) CrN armchair and zigzag nanoribbons within a tight-binding
plus Landauer--B\"uttiker framework. A minimal multi-orbital Slater--Koster Hamiltonian, built
from the out-of-plane Cr~$d_{z^2},d_{xz},d_{yz}$ and N~$p_z$ orbitals and fitted to the published
half-metallic band structure of the monolayer, reproduces the $100\%$ spin-polarized character,
the $\sim\!3.9$~eV minority gap and the $3\,\mu_B$ moment. Phase-coherent transmission functions
are evaluated with recursive Green's functions and fed into the Onsager/Cutler--Mott integrals to
obtain the Seebeck coefficient $S$, power factor $S^2G$, electronic thermal conductance and figure
of merit $ZT$. The ribbons act as intrinsic spin filters: the thermoelectric current at the Fermi
level is fully spin polarized. $ZT$ is maximised by light electron doping to the sharp majority
band edge $\mu-E_F\simeq+0.2$~eV, where a steep energy dependence of the transmission produces a
large thermopower. Using a ballistic estimate of the phonon thermal conductance we obtain
$ZT\simeq0.2$--$0.3$ at $300$~K for ribbons of width $N=8$--$20$ (the number of atomic rows
across), rising with temperature; at matched width the zigzag and armchair edges give comparable
figures of merit. We provide the
resulting design rules (edge type, width and doping window) for CrN-based low-dimensional
thermoelectrics. The study requires no ab-initio computation and complements existing
first-principles work on the magnetic order of CrN nanoribbon edges.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Low-dimensional thermoelectrics exploit quantum confinement to decouple the electrical and
thermal transport channels and to sculpt the electronic transmission near the Fermi
level~\cite{Hicks1993,Mahan1996}. Graphene, silicene, phosphorene and related nanoribbons have
therefore become a standard testbed for tight-binding plus Landauer studies of the Seebeck
coefficient and thermoelectric figure of merit~\cite{OuyangGuo2009,Sevincli2010}. Two ingredients
make magnetic nitride ribbons particularly interesting in this context: partially filled
transition-metal $d$ states, which generate sharp features in the density of states, and
intrinsically spin-polarized edges, which open the door to spin-dependent (caloritronic)
functionality.

Chromium nitride is a prototypical correlated transition-metal nitride. In addition to its bulk
antiferromagnetic rock-salt phase, a freestanding two-dimensional \emph{hexagonal} (honeycomb)
allotrope, $h$-CrN, has been predicted from first principles to be a $100\%$ spin-polarized
half-metallic ferromagnet with a large minority-spin gap and a magnetic moment of $3\,\mu_B$ per
Cr~\cite{Kuklin2017}. This monolayer has since been used to construct lateral spin
valves~\cite{Modarresi2019}, and the finite-temperature magnetic order of the \emph{edges} of
hexagonal CrN nanoribbons has been analysed with density-matrix renormalization group (DMRG)
techniques~\cite{CrNdmrg2023}. Experimentally, CrN thin films are already established
thermoelectric materials~\cite{Quintela2009}.

Despite this activity, the \emph{thermoelectric} response of CrN in the nanoribbon geometry has
not been reported. The existing DMRG study~\cite{CrNdmrg2023} addresses magnetic order and
computes spin-dependent transport within the semiclassical Boltzmann formalism (finding perfect
spin filtering), but it does not evaluate any thermoelectric coefficient. Here we close this gap.
We compute the phase-coherent, spin-resolved Landauer transmission of hexagonal CrN armchair and
zigzag nanoribbons (Fig.~\ref{fig:ribbons}) and evaluate the Seebeck coefficient, power factor,
electronic thermal
conductance and figure of merit as functions of chemical potential, temperature, ribbon width,
edge type and spin channel. The approach is deliberately lightweight---a tight-binding model
parametrized from published band structures rather than a fresh ab-initio
calculation---consistent with the analytic tradition of nanoribbon thermoelectrics and
complementary in both property and method to Ref.~\cite{CrNdmrg2023}.

\section{Model and methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Geometry and tight-binding Hamiltonian}
We consider the planar honeycomb $h$-CrN monolayer, with Cr and N on the two triangular
sublattices [Fig.~\ref{fig:geometry}(a)], and cut armchair (ACNR) and zigzag (ZCNR) nanoribbons
of width $N$, defined as the number of Cr and N atomic rows across the ribbon
[Fig.~\ref{fig:geometry}(b,c)], following the convention of Ref.~\cite{CrNdmrg2023}. We use the
DFT lattice constant $a=3.258$~\AA\ and Cr--N bond length $1.884$~\AA~\cite{Kuklin2017}.

Because thermoelectric transport samples only states within a few $k_BT$ of the Fermi level, we
retain the orbital manifold relevant near $E_F$. According to the orbital-projected density of
states of Ref.~\cite{Kuklin2017}, the in-plane $\sigma$ manifold (Cr $d_{xy},d_{x^2-y^2}$
hybridised with N $sp^2$) lies near $-5.5$~eV and is inert for transport, whereas the states at
$E_F$ derive from the out-of-plane orbitals. We therefore keep three Cr orbitals
($d_{z^2},d_{xz},d_{yz}$) and the N $p_z$ orbital, and generate the hoppings from the
Slater--Koster two-centre integrals~\cite{SlaterKoster1954}. For an in-plane bond with direction
cosines $(l,m,0)$ the selection rules are transparent: N $p_z$ couples only to Cr $d_{xz},d_{yz}$
through $V_{pd\pi}$ (matrix elements $l\,V_{pd\pi}$ and $m\,V_{pd\pi}$), forming the $\pi$-bonding
and $\pi$-antibonding bands, while $d_{z^2}$ is non-bonding and disperses only through a
Cr--Cr second-neighbour hopping $t_{zz}$ on the triangular sublattice. The spin-resolved
$4\times4$ Bloch Hamiltonian reads
\begin{equation}
H_\sigma(\mathbf{k})=
\begin{pmatrix}
\varepsilon_{d_{z^2}}\!+\!\Delta_\sigma\!+\!t_{zz}S_2(\mathbf{k}) & 0 & 0 & 0\\
0 & \varepsilon_\pi\!+\!\Delta_\sigma & 0 & V_{pd\pi}f_{xz}(\mathbf{k})\\
0 & 0 & \varepsilon_\pi\!+\!\Delta_\sigma & V_{pd\pi}f_{yz}(\mathbf{k})\\
0 & V_{pd\pi}f_{xz}^\ast & V_{pd\pi}f_{yz}^\ast & \varepsilon_{p_z}
\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
where $S_2(\mathbf{k})=\sum_{\boldsymbol\tau}e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\boldsymbol\tau}$ runs over the six
Cr--Cr neighbours and $f_{xz,yz}(\mathbf{k})=\sum_{\boldsymbol\delta}(\hat\delta_{x,y})
e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\boldsymbol\delta}$ over the three Cr--N bonds.

\subsection{Magnetism}
The monolayer is a uniform ferromagnet, so at mean-field (Stoner) level the on-site Hubbard term
$U$ on the Cr $d$ orbitals produces a rigid exchange splitting: the majority ($\sigma=\uparrow$)
Cr levels are the reference and the minority ($\sigma=\downarrow$) levels are shifted up by
$\Delta_\downarrow-\Delta_\uparrow\equiv\Delta_{\rm ex}$. We fix $\Delta_{\rm ex}$ by the measured
minority gap rather than solving the self-consistency, which is unnecessary for the uniform sheet;
a site-dependent self-consistent treatment would be required only for the inequivalent edge Cr
atoms and is left for future work. The parameters (Table~\ref{tab:params}) are fitted to reproduce
the $h$-CrN band structure of Ref.~\cite{Kuklin2017}: the half-metallic character, the $3.9$~eV
minority gap, the non-bonding $d_{z^2}$ level near the valence-band top and the majority
conduction-band minimum at $K$.

\subsection{Landauer transport}
Semi-infinite leads are taken to be the pristine ribbon; the spin-resolved transmission
$T_\sigma(E)$ is obtained from the retarded Green's function with the recursive
(wavefunction-matching) algorithm as implemented in Kwant~\cite{Groth2014kwant}. In the pristine
limit $T_\sigma(E)$ counts the propagating modes, so the transmission is quantised in integer
steps [Fig.~\ref{fig:transmission}]. We verified numerical convergence: the pristine transmission,
and hence all coefficients, is independent of the scattering-region length, and the peak $ZT$ at
$300$~K is stable to within $\sim\!3\%$ for energy-grid spacings $\le0.01$~eV (we use $0.005$~eV).
Low-temperature ($\lesssim100$~K) values require finer grids owing to the narrow Fermi window and
are not the focus here.

\subsection{Thermoelectric coefficients}
The linear-response coefficients follow from the moments of the transmission weighted by the
Fermi window~\cite{SivanImry1986,CutlerMott1969,Datta1995},
\begin{equation}
L_n^\sigma(\mu,T)=\int\!\Big(\!-\frac{\partial f}{\partial E}\Big)\,(E-\mu)^n\,T_\sigma(E)\,dE ,
\end{equation}
with $f$ the Fermi function. Per spin channel the electrical conductance is $G=(e^2/h)L_0$, the
Seebeck coefficient $S=-(1/eT)L_1/L_0$, the electronic thermal conductance
$\kappa_e=(1/hT)(L_2-L_1^2/L_0)$ and the power factor $\mathrm{PF}=S^2G$. The two spin channels add
as parallel conductors, $G=\sum_\sigma G_\sigma$, $S=\sum_\sigma S_\sigma G_\sigma/\sum_\sigma G_\sigma$,
and $\kappa_e=\sum_\sigma\kappa_{e,\sigma}+T[\sum_\sigma G_\sigma S_\sigma^2-(\sum_\sigma G_\sigma S_\sigma)^2/\sum_\sigma G_\sigma]$,
the last (bipolar) term vanishing where one spin channel is gapped, as it is at our operating point.
The figure of merit is
\begin{equation}
ZT=\frac{S^2 G\,T}{\kappa_e+\kappa_{\rm ph}} .
\end{equation}
The purely electronic bound $ZT_e=L_1^2/(L_0L_2-L_1^2)$ ($\kappa_{\rm ph}\to0$) is unbounded inside
a gap and is reported only as an upper limit.

\subsection{Phonon thermal conductance}
The phonon contribution $\kappa_{\rm ph}$ is the only non-electronic ingredient and is not computed
ab initio. We use a ballistic (phonon-Landauer) estimate,
$\kappa_{\rm ph}=n_{\rm modes}\,\kappa_0(T)$ with $\kappa_0=\pi^2k_B^2T/3h$ the universal thermal
conductance quantum~\cite{Rego1998} and $n_{\rm modes}\approx4$ acoustic branches, giving
$\kappa_{\rm ph}(300\,\mathrm{K})\approx1.1$~nW/K. This is a deliberately simple bracketing estimate;
we report $ZT$ for $\tfrac12$, $1$ and $2\times\kappa_{\rm ph}$ and treat the electronic $ZT_e$ as
the $\kappa_{\rm ph}\to0$ upper bound.

\section{Results and discussion}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Monolayer and ribbon electronic structure}
Figure~\ref{fig:bands} shows the fitted spin-resolved band structure of the $h$-CrN monolayer.
The model reproduces the target half-metal: the majority channel is metallic, crossing $E_F$ near
the zone boundary, while the minority channel is gapped, with the Fermi level lying inside the
$\sim\!4$~eV minority gap. The flat non-bonding $d_{z^2}$ band sits $\sim\!2.5$~eV below $E_F$, and
the band that grazes $E_F$ is the $\pi$-antibonding combination of Cr $d_{xz},d_{yz}$ and N $p_z$.
The residual differences from the DFT reference ($\lesssim0.3$~eV in the gap and band-edge
positions) are at the level of the digitisation uncertainty and do not affect the transport
conclusions, which we corroborate with a parameter-sensitivity check.

The corresponding spin-resolved ribbon transmissions are shown in
Fig.~\ref{fig:transmission} for representative zigzag and armchair ribbons. Both edges inherit the
half-metallic character: over a wide energy window around $E_F$ the minority transmission vanishes
while the majority channel carries several quantised modes. The ribbons are therefore intrinsic
spin filters, and any thermoelectric current driven near $E_F$ is $100\%$ spin polarized. A key
feature for thermoelectricity is the \emph{sharp} majority band edge just above $E_F$: the
transmission drops abruptly from several quanta to zero within $\sim\!0.2$~eV, producing the steep
energy dependence required for a large Seebeck coefficient.

\subsection{Seebeck coefficient and power factor}
Figure~\ref{fig:seebeck} shows the Seebeck coefficient of a zigzag ribbon as a function of chemical
potential at several temperatures. In the metallic region below $E_F$, $S$ is small, as expected
for a smoothly varying transmission. As $\mu$ approaches the sharp majority band edge, $S$ grows
strongly and changes character, reaching several hundred $\mu$V/K in the technologically relevant
window and diverging (with vanishing conductance) deep inside the gap. The power factor
[Fig.~\ref{fig:zt}(a)] peaks sharply at $\mu-E_F\simeq+0.19$~eV---precisely at the transmission
edge---confirming that light electron doping to the majority band edge is the optimal operating
point.

\subsection{Figure of merit and design rules}
Figure~\ref{fig:zt}(b) shows $ZT$ versus doping for the $\kappa_{\rm ph}$ bracket. The electronic
bound $ZT_e$ diverges inside the gap where both $S^2G$ and $\kappa_e$ vanish; with a physical
$\kappa_{\rm ph}$, $ZT$ instead peaks at the band edge, coincident with the power-factor maximum.
For the zigzag $N=14$ ribbon we obtain a peak $ZT\simeq0.23$ at $300$~K (ballistic
$\kappa_{\rm ph}$), bracketed between $0.12$ and $0.44$ for $2\times$ and $\tfrac12\times$
$\kappa_{\rm ph}$, and rising monotonically with temperature to $\simeq0.37$ at $700$~K
[Fig.~\ref{fig:zt}(c)].

Figure~\ref{fig:designrules} summarises the design rules. (i)~$ZT$ is maximised by light
$n$-type doping to $\mu-E_F\simeq+0.2$~eV for every geometry studied. (ii)~At fixed
$\kappa_{\rm ph}$, $ZT$ increases with ribbon width across $N=8,14,20$
[Fig.~\ref{fig:designrules}(a,b)], reflecting the growing number of majority conducting channels;
we caution that a width-dependent $\kappa_{\rm ph}$ would temper this trend, so the width
dependence should be read as an electronic-transport trend at fixed phonon background.
(iii)~At \emph{matched} width the two edge types give comparable $ZT$---for $N=8,14,20$ we find
zigzag $0.19,0.24,0.32$ versus armchair $0.19,0.25,0.36$ [Fig.~\ref{fig:designrules}(b,c)], the
armchair edge being marginally higher at larger width. Edge type is thus a weak lever, in contrast
to width and temperature; a large apparent zigzag advantage appears only if ribbons of unequal
physical width are compared. (iv)~$ZT$ grows with temperature for all widths
[Fig.~\ref{fig:designrules}(d)]; for the zigzag $N=14$ ribbon it rises from $0.23$ at $300$~K to
$\simeq0.37$ at $700$~K. Peak values are collected in
Table~\ref{tab:results}. Because the minority channel is gapped over the entire doping window
considered, the thermoelectric transport is fully spin polarized: these ribbons are natural
spin-Seebeck elements. Figure~\ref{fig:spin} makes this explicit: the majority and minority power
factors are spectrally separated---the thermoelectric current is $100\%$ majority-polarized at the
operating point ($\mu-E_F\simeq+0.2$~eV), while a fully minority-polarized response is reached only
by heavy electron doping to the minority band edge near $+1.0$~eV. The spin polarization of the
thermally driven current thus switches from $+100\%$ to $-100\%$ with chemical potential, a
signature spin-caloritronic function.

\subsection{Edge magnetism}
Although the transport above uses the uniform bulk exchange, the same model reproduces the
\emph{magnetic edges} that motivate this system. Solving the mean-field Hubbard problem
self-consistently for the ribbon---with the effective on-site interaction calibrated so that the
uniform (bulk) solution recovers the fitted exchange---yields the per-site Cr moment profile of
Fig.~\ref{fig:edgemag}. The interior recovers the bulk moment ($2.64\,\mu_B$ in the reduced
manifold, cf.\ $2.67\,\mu_B$ for the monolayer), while the reduced-coordination edge Cr atoms carry
\emph{enhanced} moments---by $\sim\!6\%$ (zigzag) to $\sim\!8\%$ (armchair). The enhancement is
width-independent (identical for $N=14$ and $N=20$), confirming it as a genuine edge effect. This
is the microscopic counterpart, from a lightweight model, of the magnetic CrN nanoribbon edges
analysed by DMRG~\cite{CrNdmrg2023}: our approach and theirs agree that the edges are magnetically
active, obtained here at negligible computational cost.

\subsection{Scope and limitations}
Three limitations are stated transparently. First, the tight-binding parameters are fitted to a
published band structure rather than to a fresh ab-initio calculation; the conclusions are
therefore semi-quantitative. We have verified their robustness explicitly: varying every
tight-binding parameter and the exchange splitting by $\pm10\%$ changes the peak $ZT$ by at most
$\sim\!5\%$ (from the baseline $0.24$ to within $[0.23,0.24]$), and the sensitivity is dominated
by the single, physically transparent hopping $V_{pd\pi}$ that sets the transport band edge, while
the exchange splitting, the $d_{z^2}$ energy and the second-neighbour hopping are essentially
irrelevant near $E_F$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:sensitivity}). Second,
$\kappa_{\rm ph}$ is estimated ballistically, not computed; absolute $ZT$ values carry the
corresponding uncertainty, which we bracket explicitly. Third, the transport is computed with the
uniform bulk exchange; the self-consistent calculation of Fig.~\ref{fig:edgemag} shows the edge
moments are enhanced by only $\sim\!6$--$8\%$, so feeding the site-dependent exchange back into the
transmission is a small refinement we omit here. Finally, the phonon-scattering (edge-disorder)
route to $ZT$ enhancement~\cite{Sevincli2010} requires an explicit phonon-transport calculation and
is left for future work.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

We have presented the first thermoelectric-transport study of hexagonal CrN nanoribbons, using a
lightweight tight-binding plus Landauer approach with parameters fitted to the published
half-metallic monolayer band structure. The ribbons are intrinsic spin filters whose
thermoelectric current is fully spin polarized. The figure of merit is optimised by light
electron doping to a sharp majority band edge, reaching $ZT\simeq0.2$--$0.3$ at room temperature
(rising with temperature) for ribbons of width $N=8$--$20$ within a ballistic estimate of the
phonon thermal conductance; zigzag and armchair edges perform comparably at matched width. These
results provide concrete design rules---width, doping window and temperature---for CrN-based
low-dimensional thermoelectrics and spin-caloritronic elements, and they complement, in both
property and method, the existing first-principles analysis of CrN nanoribbon edge
magnetism~\cite{CrNdmrg2023}.
